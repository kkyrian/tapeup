The first release of TapeUp, a meeting recorder for Mac built for you and your AI agent.

## What it does

- **Records any call.** One click records your voice and everything your Mac plays, in any app. No bot joins the call. Press ⌥⌘R to start recording from any app, and small controls float over your windows to pause or stop.
- **Transcribes on your Mac** while you talk, in 52 languages.
- **Finds who said what.** Click **Label speakers**, and TapeUp finds each voice and shows a line it said. Play a voice to hear who it is.
- **Lists who was there.** When you stop, tap how many people were on the call and type the first letters of each name.
- **Titles your recordings.** Choose Claude Code or Codex in Settings, and it titles each recording and suggests who was there. It's off until you choose one. Without it, Apple Intelligence titles recordings on Macs that have it.
- **Imports old recordings** from files or folders and transcribes them, with progress and the time left.
- **Finds any meeting** by its title, the people in it, or anything that was said.
- **Works with your agent.** Each recording is a folder of plain files with an `AGENTS.md`, and agents can use TapeUp's command line.
- **Keeps recording** when AirPods connect or disconnect mid-call. Audio is saved as it records, so you keep the meeting even if TapeUp crashes.

Only the agent you choose sees a transcript.

## Install

Download **TapeUp.dmg** below and drag TapeUp to Applications, or:

```
brew install kkyrian/tap/tapeup
```

TapeUp isn't notarized by Apple, so macOS stops it the first time. Click **Done**, then **Open Anyway** next to TapeUp in System Settings › Privacy & Security. It downloads its speech model once (2.3 GB, or 680 MB on Macs with less than 16 GB of memory).

Needs a Mac with Apple silicon and macOS 15 or later; tested on macOS 27.

## Updates

TapeUp checks once a day and installs updates when you're not recording. Turn that off under Settings › Automatic updates, or use **Check for Updates…** in the ··· menu.
